# Policy Statement PS26/7: Financial crime controls for customer due diligence and transaction monitoring

Financial Services Oversight Authority (FSOA). Published November 2026. This is a fictional document created for training purposes.

## 1. Introduction and purpose

1.1 This policy statement sets out final rules and guidance on customer due diligence and transaction monitoring for firms authorised by the Financial Services Oversight Authority. It follows Consultation Paper CP26/3, which closed in July 2026.

1.2 Our supervisory work found that many firms screen customers only by name against sanctions and politically exposed person (PEP) lists. These firms often miss customers who are connected to a PEP but are not named on any list. We also found that firms set transaction monitoring rules once at onboarding and do not change them when what they know about a customer changes.

1.3 Our objective is that a firm's view of a customer's risk is kept up to date, is used consistently by its onboarding and monitoring controls, and can be explained to a reviewer by reference to the evidence.

1.4 This policy statement takes effect on 1 March 2027. The deadlines for individual requirements are set out in the relevant sections, and transitional arrangements are set out in section 9.

## 2. Scope and application

2.1 This policy statement applies to all banks, building societies, payment institutions and electronic money institutions authorised by the FSOA.

2.2 It applies to all retail customers who are individuals. Requirements for corporate customers will be the subject of a separate consultation.

2.3 Some requirements are applied proportionately. For the purposes of this policy statement, a small firm is a firm that has fewer than 250,000 customers and total assets of less than £5 billion on 31 December 2026. A firm that exceeds either threshold is not a small firm.

2.4 Firms may use automated tools, including artificial intelligence, to meet these requirements. The firm remains responsible for every decision, and the requirements for human review in sections 5 and 6 apply however a decision is made.

## 3. Definitions

3.1 A politically exposed person (PEP) is an individual who holds, or has held in the last 12 months, a prominent public function, such as a head of state, a government minister, a member of parliament, a senior judge, a central bank governor or an ambassador.

3.2 A close associate of a PEP is an individual who has a known joint business ownership, business partnership or other close business relationship with a PEP. A family member of a PEP is the PEP's spouse or partner, child, or parent.

3.3 A high-risk third country is a country on the FSOA's published list. At the date of this policy statement the list is Valdoria, Karvonia and Ostmark.

3.4 A high-risk customer is a customer to whom the firm has applied enhanced due diligence under section 4, or whom the firm has otherwise rated as high risk under section 5.

3.5 An alert is any transaction that a firm's monitoring blocks, or refers for human review.

3.6 Business days are days other than weekends and public holidays in our jurisdiction.

## 4. Enhanced due diligence

4.1 Firms must apply enhanced due diligence (EDD) to any customer who is a PEP, a family member of a PEP or a close associate of a PEP, as defined in paragraphs 3.1 and 3.2.

4.2 Firms must apply EDD to any customer who is a national of, or resident in, a high-risk third country, as defined in paragraph 3.3.

4.3 Firms must identify close associates and family members from the information the customer gives in their application, as well as from screening. A customer who states a business relationship with a person on a PEP list must be treated as a close associate.

4.4 Where a customer's expected initial deposit is more than £100,000, or the equivalent in another currency, the firm must obtain evidence of the source of funds before the account is opened.

4.5 A customer whose name matches an entry on a sanctions list must not be onboarded until a human reviewer has confirmed or discounted the match. A confirmed match must be rejected.

4.6 EDD must be approved by a senior manager before the account is opened.

## 5. Customer risk rating

5.1 Firms must give every customer a risk rating of low, medium or high when the account is opened. A customer to whom EDD applies under section 4 must be rated high.

5.2 Firms must record the reasons for each rating and the paragraph of this policy statement, or of the firm's own policy, on which each reason relies.

5.3 Firms must make the customer's current risk rating available to their transaction monitoring controls. A rating that is held only by the onboarding team does not meet this requirement.

5.4 Firms must review the risk rating of every high-risk customer at least every 12 months, and of every medium-risk customer at least every 24 months.

5.5 Firms must re-rate a customer within 10 business days of a trigger event. Trigger events include a change in the customer's PEP status and the alerts described in paragraph 6.5.

## 6. Transaction monitoring

6.1 Firms must monitor every card transaction against the customer's usual behaviour and their current risk rating under section 5.

6.2 For high-risk customers, a card payment made outside the customer's home country that is larger than the customer's usual maximum amount must not be approved automatically. It must be referred to a human reviewer.

6.3 For high-risk customers, firms must refer to a human reviewer any transaction whose automated risk score is 20 or more on a scale of 0 to 100, or whose automated decision is made with a confidence below 0.85.

6.4 Payments to cryptocurrency exchanges, gift card sellers and online gambling operators are high-risk merchant categories. For any customer, a payment of more than £250 to a high-risk merchant category must be referred to a human reviewer.

6.5 Where a customer has 2 or more alerts within any 30-day period, the firm must treat this as a trigger event under paragraph 5.5 and re-rate the customer.

6.6 When a human reviewer explains an alert, for example by confirming that the customer is travelling, firms must record the explanation and take it into account when monitoring later transactions.

## 7. Suspicious activity reporting

7.1 Where a firm knows or suspects that a transaction involves the proceeds of crime, it must submit a suspicious activity report to the FSOA's financial intelligence unit within 2 business days.

7.2 Firms must not tell the customer that a report has been made or is being considered.

7.3 Firms must keep records of every alert, the reviewer's decision and the reasons for it, for at least 5 years.

## 8. Governance and accountability

8.1 Firms must name a senior manager who is responsible for financial crime controls, and must notify the FSOA of that person by 1 March 2027.

8.2 Firms must test their customer due diligence and transaction monitoring controls at least once a year, including tests of automated tools against cases with known outcomes. The first test must be completed by 30 September 2027.

8.3 The results of each test must be reported to the board within 3 months of the test.

## 9. Transitional arrangements

9.1 For customers onboarded before 1 March 2027, firms must identify close associates and family members of PEPs under paragraph 4.3, and apply EDD under paragraph 4.1, by 30 June 2027.

9.2 Small firms, as defined in paragraph 2.3, may meet paragraph 9.1 by 31 December 2027 instead of 30 June 2027.

9.3 Small firms may complete their first test under paragraph 8.2 by 31 March 2028 instead of 30 September 2027.

9.4 The transitional arrangements do not apply to paragraphs 4.5, 6.2, 6.3 or 8.1. These apply to all firms from 1 March 2027.

## 10. Next steps and contacts

10.1 We will update the list of high-risk third countries in paragraph 3.3 at least every 6 months and publish each update on our website.

10.2 Firms with questions about this policy statement should contact their usual supervisory contact.
