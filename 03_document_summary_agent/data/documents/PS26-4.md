# Policy Statement PS26/4: Operational resilience and third-party technology risk

Financial Services Oversight Authority (FSOA). Published October 2026. This is a fictional document created for training purposes.

## 1. Introduction and purpose

1.1 This policy statement sets out the final rules and guidance on operational resilience and the management of third-party technology risk for firms authorised by the Financial Services Oversight Authority. It follows Consultation Paper CP25/11, which closed in March 2026, and reflects the feedback we received from firms, trade bodies and consumer groups.

1.2 Disruption to financial services can cause serious harm to consumers and to market integrity. Recent incidents have shown that firms increasingly depend on a small number of technology providers, and that an outage at one provider can affect many firms at the same time. Firms have also told us that they find it hard to assess the resilience of services they do not run themselves.

1.3 Our objective is that firms can continue to deliver their most important services through severe but plausible disruption, and that when disruption does happen, it is contained, reported promptly and learned from. We expect firms to treat operational resilience as a matter for the board, not only for technology teams.

1.4 This policy statement takes effect on 1 January 2027. The deadlines for individual requirements are set out in the relevant sections, and transitional arrangements for some firms are set out in section 9.

1.5 We will review the effectiveness of these rules two years after they take effect, and we will publish our findings.

## 2. Scope and application

2.1 This policy statement applies to all banks, building societies, payment institutions and electronic money institutions authorised by the FSOA.

2.2 It applies to the firm's activities in all jurisdictions where the failure of an activity could cause harm to consumers in our jurisdiction or to the stability of our financial system.

2.3 Some requirements are applied proportionately. For the purposes of this policy statement, a small firm is a firm that has fewer than 250,000 customers and total assets of less than £5 billion on 31 December 2026. A firm that exceeds either threshold is not a small firm.

2.4 A firm that becomes a small firm, or stops being a small firm, after 31 December 2026 must notify the FSOA within 30 days of the change. Its status for the purposes of the transitional arrangements in section 9 is fixed on 31 December 2026 and does not change.

2.5 Groups may meet these requirements at group level, provided that each authorised firm in the group can show how the group arrangements apply to its own important business services.

## 3. Definitions

3.1 An important business service is a service that a firm provides to an external end user, where disruption to the service could cause intolerable harm to consumers or risk to market integrity. Internal services, such as human resources or internal reporting, are not important business services on their own.

3.2 An impact tolerance is the maximum tolerable level of disruption to an important business service, expressed as a maximum length of time and, where relevant, a maximum number of affected customers or transactions.

3.3 A material third-party provider is a provider of technology services whose failure would cause the firm to breach an impact tolerance for one or more important business services.

3.4 A major incident is an operational incident that has caused, or is likely to cause, a breach of an impact tolerance, or that affects more than 10% of the firm's customers.

3.5 Business days are days other than weekends and public holidays in our jurisdiction.

## 4. Important business services and impact tolerances

4.1 Firms must identify their important business services by 31 March 2027. In doing so, firms should consider the number and type of customers using each service, the availability of alternatives, and the time sensitivity of payments or other transactions.

4.2 Firms must set an impact tolerance for each important business service by 31 March 2027. Impact tolerances must be set at the point at which harm becomes intolerable, not at the point at which the firm expects to be able to recover.

4.3 The board must approve the list of important business services and their impact tolerances, and must review both at least once every 12 months, or sooner if there is a material change to the firm's business.

4.4 We expect most retail banks to identify between 6 and 15 important business services. Firms that identify fewer than 4 should be prepared to explain why.

4.5 Firms must keep a record of how they identified each important business service and set each impact tolerance, and must provide it to the FSOA on request within 10 business days.

## 5. Mapping and scenario testing

5.1 Firms must map the people, processes, technology, facilities and information that support each important business service, including those provided by third parties, by 31 March 2027.

5.2 Mapping must be detailed enough to identify single points of failure and concentrations of risk, including where several important business services depend on the same third-party provider.

5.3 Firms must test their ability to remain within impact tolerance under severe but plausible scenarios. The first round of scenario testing must be completed by 30 September 2027, and testing must be repeated at least once a year after that.

5.4 Scenarios must include, at a minimum, the failure of a material third-party provider, a cyber attack that makes key systems unavailable, and the loss of a key site.

5.5 Where testing shows that a firm cannot remain within an impact tolerance, the firm must record the vulnerability, set out the actions it will take to address it, and report the vulnerability to the board at its next meeting.

## 6. Third-party technology providers

6.1 Firms must maintain a register of all material third-party providers by 31 March 2027. The register must record, for each provider, the services it supports, the data it holds, its location, and the date the contract expires.

6.2 Firms must notify the FSOA at least 60 days before entering into a new arrangement with a material third-party provider, or materially changing an existing one.

6.3 Contracts with material third-party providers must give the firm and the FSOA rights of access and audit, require the provider to report incidents to the firm without undue delay, and set out how the service will be transferred or wound down if the arrangement ends.

6.4 Firms must have a documented exit plan for each material third-party provider and must test each exit plan at least once every two years.

6.5 Where a firm uses the same material third-party provider for more than one important business service, it must assess the concentration risk and explain to the board how it would manage the failure of that provider.

6.6 Firms remain fully responsible for meeting their regulatory obligations when they outsource. Outsourcing a service does not transfer the firm's responsibility for it.

## 7. Incident reporting

7.1 Firms must submit an initial notification to the FSOA within 4 hours of classifying an incident as a major incident, and in any case no later than 24 hours after the incident is first detected.

7.2 Firms must submit an intermediate report within 72 hours of the initial notification, setting out the cause of the incident as far as it is known, the services and customers affected, and the actions taken so far.

7.3 Firms must submit a final report within 30 business days of the incident being resolved. The final report must include a root-cause analysis and the lessons learned.

7.4 Where a major incident is caused by a third-party provider, the firm remains responsible for reporting it. Firms should not delay a notification while they wait for information from the provider.

7.5 We will publish an incident reporting template before the rules take effect. Until then, firms may use their existing incident reporting formats.

## 8. Governance and accountability

8.1 Firms must name a senior manager who is responsible for operational resilience by 31 January 2027, and must notify the FSOA of that person's name within 14 days of appointing them.

8.2 Firms must complete an annual self-assessment of their operational resilience, which must be approved by the board. The first self-assessment must be completed and approved by 31 March 2028.

8.3 The self-assessment must cover the firm's important business services, impact tolerances, mapping, scenario testing results, vulnerabilities and remediation actions, and its management of material third-party providers.

8.4 Boards should receive regular management information on operational resilience, at least quarterly, and should challenge it.

8.5 Firms must keep each self-assessment for at least 5 years and provide it to the FSOA on request.

## 9. Transitional arrangements

9.1 Small firms, as defined in paragraph 2.3, may meet the requirements in paragraphs 4.1, 4.2, 5.1 and 6.1 by 31 March 2028 instead of 31 March 2027.

9.2 Small firms may complete their first round of scenario testing under paragraph 5.3 by 30 September 2028 instead of 30 September 2027.

9.3 For all firms, contracts with material third-party providers that were in place before 1 January 2027 must be updated to meet paragraph 6.3 at their next renewal, or by 31 December 2028, whichever is earlier.

9.4 The transitional arrangements do not apply to the incident reporting requirements in section 7 or to the requirement to name a senior manager in paragraph 8.1. These apply to all firms from 1 January 2027.

## 10. Next steps and contacts

10.1 We will host a series of webinars for firms in the first quarter of 2027 and will publish frequently asked questions on our website.

10.2 Firms with questions about this policy statement should contact their usual supervisory contact. General questions can be sent to our operational resilience team.

10.3 We will consult separately in 2027 on whether to extend these requirements to insurers and investment firms.
